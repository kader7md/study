You can miss 15 questions and still pass. It is a computer test (CBT). You can flag a question and come back to it at the end.

Since July 2025 there are 4 question formats

  1. One sentence, 正 or 誤. (in your practice test)
  2. Two sentences ア・イ, choose the 正誤 combination A–D. (in your practice test)
  3. Two sentences with blanks (穴埋め), choose the word combination. New. Not in your practice test.
  4. Three sentences, choose the one that is 正しい (correct) or 誤っている (wrong). New. Not in your practice test.

The insurance association says the exam became harder with this change. The official study site (you get the login ID after you apply) has a mock exam in the new format. Do it at least once.

Can you pass with tricks only?

I tested a "keyword rule" on all 182 questions of your practice test. The rule does not read the content. It only looks for a few words:

Keyword rule: answer 誤 if the sentence has いっさい, 必ず, すべて, のみ (but not のみならず), 全額, 限り, 常に, or 必要はありません. Otherwise answer 正.

What to do for each format

① One sentence 正・誤

  1. Look at the ending only (last 10 characters).
  2. Scan for the trap words: いっさい・必ず・すべて・のみ・全額・限り・必要はありません.
  3. Do you recognize a topic from the false list? Use the fact.
  4. Nothing found → 正. Move on. Don't spend more than 40 seconds.

② ア・イ combination A–D

  1. Judge ア and イ separately with method ①.
  2. Not sure about one? Treat it as 正.
  3. D (both false) is rare. In your practice test it was correct only once in 27. A was 10, B was 9, C was 7.
  4. If both look true, answer A.

③ Fill-in-the-blank (穴埋め)

  1. The blanks are almost always key words: who (保険会社 / 代理店 / 保険契約者 / 被保険者), a number, or a law name.
  2. Learn the Words tab by shape: you only need to recognize them, not read every kanji.
  3. Remove choices that make the sentence absolute (いっさい, 全額). Then choose.

④ Three sentences, choose one

  1. First read the question: 正しいもの = choose the TRUE one. 誤っているもの = choose the FALSE one.
  2. Choosing the FALSE one: pick the sentence with a trap word or "必要はありません".
  3. Choosing the TRUE one: pick the sentence with soft words (原則として, 一般的に, など) and no trap word.

Two kinds of questions

1. 正誤せいご (true / false), one sentence

Read the sentence. If it is correct, answer 正せい (true). If any part is wrong, answer 誤ご (false). One wrong word makes the whole sentence false.

2. 組合くみあわせ (combination), two sentences ア and イ

次の記述の正誤の組合せとして、正しいものを選んでください。

"Choose the correct true/false combination for the following sentences."

Decide ア true or false, then イ true or false. Then pick the letter:

Aア ○ trueイ ○ true Bア ○ trueイ × false Cア × falseイ ○ true Dア × falseイ × false

Tip: judge ア and イ separately first, as two true/false questions. Only then look at A to D.

Rule 1: when you really don't know, answer 正 (true). Most sentences in this test are true. The false ones are made by changing one or two words in a true sentence. So your job is to find the changed word.

Why you can't just read left to right

In Japanese, the verb and the "not" come at the very end. The last few characters decide "can" or "cannot", "must" or "does not need to". Here is a real sentence from your test, split into parts:

"Tort liability under the Civil Code arises only when the two sides already have a contract." (Question 69-3)

Answer: 誤 false. The word のみ (only) is the trap. Tort liability happens even with no contract, for example when you crash into a stranger's car. Without のみ, the sentence would still be wrong, so the writer added a strong word on purpose.

Who / what (before は・が) Condition (場合, とき, ても) Strong word (the trap) Ending: decides the meaning

The 5-step reading method

  1. Read the ending first. Jump to the last part after the final 、. Is it なければなりません (must), てはなりません (must not), 必要はありません (not needed), できません (cannot)? Use the table below.
  2. Find WHO. The word before は or が. This test often swaps 代理店 (agent) and 保険会社 (insurance company), or 保険契約者 (policyholder) and 被保険者 (insured).
  3. Circle numbers. 10日, 30%〜50%, 5万円, 70:30. One changed number makes it false.
  4. Look for strong words. いっさい, 必ず, すべて, のみ, 全額. Ask: "Is there an exception?" If yes, it is false.
  5. Check direction. 含まれる ↔ 含まれない, 対象となる ↔ 対象とならない, 必要 ↔ 不要. The test flips one word and keeps the rest.

Length and form

Long sentence with careful details (原則として, 一般的に, など, 〜に応じて): usually copied from the textbook, so it leans 正.

Sentence with one strong or negative word (いっさい, 全額, のみ, 必要はありません, 含まれません): leans 誤. One word was added to break a true sentence.

Sentence with an excuse (〜のため, 〜であることから, "because it is privacy", "because it is the company's job"): be careful. The excuse is often invented to make a wrong rule sound reasonable.

These are tendencies, not rules. Use them only when you don't know the fact.
